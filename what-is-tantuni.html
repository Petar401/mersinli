A guide to the dish

What is tantuni? Mersin's favourite wrap.

Finely chopped meat, a hot steel pan and thin lavash. Here's everything you need to know about Turkey's great street food, from the people who cook it in Norwich.

By Mersinli Tantuni by ChobanUpdated September 20263 min read

Tantuni is a hot, thin wrap from Mersin, a port city on the Mediterranean coast of southern Turkey. Small pieces of meat are simmered until tender, then fried on a sac, a wide and shallow steel pan, with chilli flakes. The meat is spooned into thin lavash bread with tomato, onion tossed in sumac and a handful of parsley, then rolled up tight and eaten hot.

It's one of the most loved street foods in Turkey, and it is still very much a Mersin thing. Ask anyone from the city where to eat it and you'll get a strong opinion.

Where tantuni comes from

Tantuni grew up on the streets of Mersin, and people there have been eating it from small stalls and counters since around the 1940s. It was fast, cheap and filling: food for the port, the market and the late night. Where the name comes from is still argued about, and every tantuni maker in Mersin seems to have their own version of the story.

Tantuni spread across Turkey from there, but the Mersin way of making it has stayed the same: meat chopped small, a very hot sac, thin bread and a few sharp, fresh things to cut through the richness.

How tantuni is made

  1. Chop. The meat is cut into very small pieces, smaller than you'd expect.
  2. Simmer. It's poached first, so it's tender all the way through.
  3. Fry on the sac. The sac has a raised dome in the middle and a shallow well around the edge. The meat is pushed onto the hot part to crisp up, with chilli flakes and a little oil.
  4. Wipe the bread. The lavash is pressed onto the pan to pick up the juices, so the wrap is never dry.
  5. Fill and roll. Meat, tomato, sumac onions and parsley go in, and it's rolled tight.

The lavash is wiped across the pan before it's filled. That's why tantuni is never dry.

Tantuni and doner: what's the difference?

People often ask us if tantuni is a kind of doner. It isn't. Both are Turkish and both come in bread, but they are cooked in very different ways.

How to eat it like they do in Mersin

  • Squeeze lemon over it before every bite, not just the first one.
  • Have the pickled peppers on the side.
  • Drink şalgam, a sour and spicy fermented carrot drink. If that's a step too far, cold ayran works too.
  • Eat it straight away. Tantuni is best in the first few minutes.
